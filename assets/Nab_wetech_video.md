**[SDLC IMPORTANCE - 1 minute]**

The Software Development Life Cycle isn't bureaucracy—it's the difference between elegant systems and expensive failures. Think of SDLC as quality gates: Requirements prevent building the wrong thing. Design catches architectural flaws before they're coded into concrete. Testing finds bugs before users do. Deployment ensures reliability. Maintenance keeps systems healthy.


At my final project in HCMUT, I developed a renting and buying book system. Following SDLC—writing requirement documents, designing database architecture, implementing incremental features, thorough testing—resulted in zero critical bugs at launch. SDLC transforms ideas into **maintainable, scalable, reliable** solutions. Without it, you're building castles on sand.

---

**[SKIPPING SDLC STEPS - 1 minute]**

What happens when you skip SDLC steps? Disaster compounds. Skip requirements? You build what users don't need. Skip design? You create technical debt that cripples future development. Skip testing? Users become your testers—and they're unforgiving. Skip security reviews? You expose sensitive data.


At Vietsovpetro, I directly witnessed the consequences of another team skipping the testing phase. They delivered a important project for all 4,000 users but published it before completing testing and certainly never tested handling 3,000 concurrent requests in a single morning. As a result, the company incurred significant financial losses. One of the reason is, The project owner wanted to rush the deployment out of fear that delaying it would make matters worse, while the team's project manager was overly optimistic about systems.



SDLC is our professional responsibility, our commitment to excellence. It's the invisible work that makes great software look effortless.
